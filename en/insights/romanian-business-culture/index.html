Romanian Business Culture: What Foreign Companies Need to Know

Entering the Romanian market is not only a legal and financial process. How a company builds relationships, communicates and makes decisions locally can shape whether the operation actually succeeds.

For foreign companies, understanding Romanian business culture is therefore not a soft skill. It is a practical part of market entry.

Relationships matter. Romanian business culture tends to value personal trust alongside formal agreements. Deals and partnerships often develop faster, and run more smoothly, once a genuine working relationship has been established — not only a signed contract.

Communication is usually direct but relationship oriented. Local partners, suppliers and employees often expect regular, personal contact rather than purely formal, written communication from a distance.

Decision making can also differ from what headquarters expects. Many Romanian organizations still operate with a fairly clear hierarchy, where decisions are made by senior figures rather than through broad consensus. Foreign companies that understand this can move faster, rather than waiting for processes that may not exist locally in the way they are used to.

Working with local employees, suppliers and partners requires the same attention. Recruitment and onboarding set the tone early, and local professionals often expect clear expectations alongside genuine investment in the working relationship.

One of the most common gaps is between headquarters expectations and local execution. International management may expect processes and reporting identical to other markets, while the Romanian team is still building the basic infrastructure needed to operate. Bridging that gap requires someone who understands both sides.

This is where local knowledge becomes a real business advantage, not just a cultural nicety. Companies that invest in understanding how things actually work in Romania tend to avoid common misunderstandings, build stronger local teams and move faster from entry to a functioning operation.

Market entry, in other words, is not only about structure. It is also about how a company operates on the ground, starting from day one. Our local management services help bridge exactly that gap.
