Why Romania? When Does It Make Sense to Build Your Next European Operation Here?

When an international company considers a new European location, Romania is rarely evaluated in isolation.

Management may also be looking at Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria, the Czech Republic or other markets in Central and Eastern Europe.

So the relevant question is not simply: Is Romania a good place to do business?

It is: Does Romania make sense for what we are trying to build?

For many companies, one of Romania’s most important advantages is the combination of access to the European Union and a substantial professional workforce.

Romania has developed significant business and technology centres in Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Iași and Timișoara, with professionals working across technology, engineering, finance, shared services and other international business functions.

Many have already worked in multinational environments.

That matters.

When an international company establishes a new operation, technical skills are only part of the equation. Employees also need to communicate with international teams, understand global corporate expectations and work effectively across cultures and time zones.

Cost is another factor.

Romania can provide a competitive operating environment compared with many Western European markets, particularly when companies consider the combined cost of talent, office space and business services.

But cost alone should never determine a market-entry decision.

The cheapest location is not necessarily the location that creates the most value.

Talent availability, infrastructure, management capability, connectivity and the ability to scale should all be considered together.

Romania’s location can also make it useful as a base for companies with broader European ambitions.

Operating within the EU framework while maintaining connections with Central, Eastern and Southeastern Europe gives companies several potential strategic options.

But perhaps Romania’s most interesting advantage is less visible on a spreadsheet.

There is already a significant ecosystem of international companies, local professionals and business-service providers operating here.

A company entering Romania is therefore not starting in an undeveloped business environment.

The infrastructure exists.

The question is how to use it effectively.

That is why the decision to expand a business to Romania should begin with the company’s objective rather than a generic list of advantages.

If the goal is to build a skilled team, establish a European operation, develop a new market or create a platform for further growth, Romania deserves serious consideration.

The opportunity may be here.

Turning that opportunity into a successful operation is the next step.
