Setting Up a Company in Romania Is the Easy Part. Building the Operation Is What Matters.

Registering a company is often treated as the starting point of international expansion.

And technically, it is.

But there is an important difference between setting up a company in Romania and establishing a Romanian operation that actually works.

A legal entity does not recruit employees.

It does not find customers, manage suppliers, set up internal processes or create the connection between the local team and international headquarters.

Those things happen after incorporation, and they are usually where the real work begins.

For an international company, establishing operations in Romania typically involves several parallel decisions.

Who will handle accounting and payroll? Which bank should the company work with? Where will employees work? Who will recruit the first team members? Which local suppliers are reliable? How will contracts and administrative requirements be managed?

And perhaps most importantly: who is responsible for making sure all of these pieces work together?

This becomes particularly important during the first months of an operation.

The first employees shape the culture of the local team. The first suppliers establish how the business operates. Early processes often become the processes the company continues using as it grows.

Small decisions made during the setup stage can therefore have consequences much later.

International headquarters also needs visibility.

Management should know what is happening locally, what decisions are required and where problems are developing, without becoming involved in every administrative detail.

That requires someone on the ground who understands both sides.

They need to understand how business works in Romania while also understanding the expectations, reporting standards and operating culture of an international organization.

This is why business setup in Romania should be viewed as more than company formation.

The legal entity is the structure.

The people, processes, suppliers, infrastructure and management around it create the business.

And as the operation grows, the requirements change again.

A five-person team does not require the same structure as a fifty-person operation. Processes that work during launch may need to become more formal. New managers may be required. Financial controls, reporting and infrastructure may need to evolve.

The objective from the beginning should therefore be not simply to establish a company, but to create an operation capable of scaling.

Because opening the company is only the beginning.
