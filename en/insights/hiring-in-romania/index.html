Hiring in Romania: What International Companies Should Know Before Building a Local Team

Romania has become an established location for international companies building teams in technology, engineering, finance, operations and business services.

But access to talent does not mean that hiring is automatic.

One of the first mistakes an international company can make when hiring in Romania is assuming that the recruitment strategy used in another country can simply be replicated locally.

The job description may be identical.

The market is not.

Salary expectations, candidate availability, employer reputation, benefits and competition vary significantly between roles and locations.

Even within Romania, hiring conditions can differ between Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Iași and Timișoara.

That makes the first stage of recruitment particularly important: understanding the market before launching a large number of vacancies.

Which profiles are readily available?

Which are difficult to recruit?

What compensation levels are realistic?

What are competing employers offering?

And what will make a candidate choose a new international company that may not yet have an established employer brand in Romania?

There is another question that is even more important.

Who should you hire first?

When building a new operation, the first employees have an influence that extends far beyond their individual jobs.

They help establish working practices, communicate the company’s culture locally and often participate in recruiting the employees who follow them.

For that reason, the strongest CV is not always enough.

Early hires need to be comfortable working in an environment that is still being built. They need to communicate effectively with international headquarters and, in many cases, operate with greater independence than they would inside an established local organization.

Recruitment is therefore connected directly to the broader market-entry strategy.

If a company expects to grow from five employees to fifty, it needs to think about management structure, onboarding, internal communication and culture long before employee number fifty arrives.

This is particularly important for hybrid and international teams.

The Romanian operation should develop its own strength without becoming disconnected from headquarters.

Good onboarding, clear responsibilities and regular communication help create that connection.

Ultimately, successful recruitment in Romania is not about filling vacancies as quickly as possible.

It is about building the team that the business will need next.

Over time, those people become the local operation.
